# 📰 AI News Summary: "AI伪造图骗‘仅退款’事件频发 如何让有图依然有真相？" (CCTV, May 17, 2026)

## 🔹 Key Issue
AI-generated fake images are increasingly used in e-commerce to fraudulently claim “only refund” (no return required). Examples include AI-made photos of "wilted fruit plants", "cracked phone cases", or "broken bracelets" — visually plausible but physically/logically impossible.

## 🔹 Real Impact
- A fig tree seller received an AI-forged image of yellowed, lifeless seedlings — despite shipping healthy, potted plants with moist soil. The platform approved the refund within minutes.
- Many small merchants report similar losses, leading some to exit platforms entirely.

## 🔹 Detection Breakthrough
✅ **National Anti-Fraud Center App** has launched an AI Content Authentication feature — capable of detecting AI-generated images & videos.

✅ **Ant Group’s system** uses a dual-model approach:
   - **Small models**: Learn statistical fingerprints left by AIGC tools (e.g., Stable Diffusion, DALL·E);
   - **Large models**: Analyze semantic/logical consistency (e.g., "a dog singing" or wilted leaves that defy botany).
→ Achieves **>90% detection recall** and **<0.01% false alarm rate**.

## 🔹 Expert Insight
- Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications: This behavior constitutes *civil fraud*, undermining trust in digital commerce.
- Detection tools empower sellers with objective evidence for dispute resolution and deter malicious actors.

## 💡 Bottom Line
"Having an image" no longer equals "having truth." As AI forgery becomes trivial, AI-powered verification is becoming essential infrastructure — not just for security, but for fair digital trade.
